# ISO 27001 Certification, Led End to End

![ISO 27001 Certification Case Study](images/iso-27001-certification.png)

## Context

The agency was a fully remote team of 30+, with people working across the world, serving clients in aerospace and defense manufacturing. That client base meant the data moving through the agency was highly confidential, and those clients needed assurance it was handled to a recognized standard.

ISO 27001 was the answer, the international standard for information security management. The agency had solid working practices already, but not in the form a certification audit requires.

Someone had to turn how the team actually worked into a documented, auditable information security management system, and coordinate the whole certification through to a pass.

That was me.

## The problem

The agency worked to a real standard day-to-day, but "we do it properly" is not something an auditor can certify.

The gap was structural.

- SOPs existed but not as the formal mapped policies ISO 27001 requires.
- Confidential client data (aerospace and defense) moved through a fully remote, globally distributed team.
- Certification required meeting defined requirements and proving them to an external body on a deadline.
- No internal security function existed to own the process end-to-end.

---

## Goal

Achieve ISO 27001 certification while building a system the team could realistically maintain afterward rather than a temporary audit project.

---

## My approach

1. Reviewed existing SOPs and working practices against ISO 27001 requirements.
2. Documented and reformatted processes into the required policies, records, and supporting documentation.
3. Ensured documentation accurately reflected how the distributed team handled confidential information.
4. Coordinated directly with the certification body throughout review cycles and evidence requests.
5. Managed the certification process through a successful first-time audit.

## Why this matters for AI Governance

ISO 27001 and AI governance share the same discipline.

A governance system requires:

- Knowing where sensitive information exists
- Documenting controls
- Demonstrating accountability
- Producing evidence when customers, investors, or auditors request it

The same operational thinking applies directly to AI governance.
